## 1. What we learned

### 1a. Maar's published claim reproduces on Llama 3.2 3B

With Maar's exact protocol AND Maar's actual prompts:
- baseline hit_rate (`-ee` family, 20 eval prompts) = **60%**
- steered hit_rate (layer 22, m=1.5, raw direction, generated-couplet metric, 25 tokens) = **30%**
- 2×2 contingency: HIT→HIT=6, HIT→MISS=**6**, MISS→HIT=**0**, MISS→MISS=8 — **all 6 flips are HIT→MISS, none are MISS→HIT**.  This is *exactly* Maar's "steered substantially lower than unsteered" claim.
- 12/20 generated texts changed under steering (60%), so the effect is causal and broad.
- direction L2 norm = 11.47 (raw mean-difference); effective steering magnitude = `m × ||d|| = 1.5 × 11.47 = 17.2` ≈ 25% of typical mid-layer residual norm.

### 1b. Why the prompts matter (and the paper doesn't tell you)

Maar's test prompts are **complete first lines ending in a rhyme word + `\n`** (e.g. `"The stars above shine bright and free\n"`).  The metric checks whether the *generated second line* (greedy continuation, 25 tokens, first 3 lines cleaned, last word, family-checked) is in the rhyme family.  This is a **rhyme-continuation task** ("model writes a second line that rhymes with the first").

Our candle-mi-authored prompts were structured differently: stems that end MID-LINE before the rhyme word (e.g. `"A rhyming couplet:\nI saw a robin in the apple"`), expecting the model to complete the FIRST line with a rhyme word.  Different task.

**None of this structural detail is in the paper text.**  See §3 below for the full list of paper-vs-code gaps.

### 1c. Maar's protocol ≠ our prolepsis protocol

Maar and our COLM paper answer **different scientific questions**:

| Dimension | Maar | Our prolepsis paper |
|---|---|---|
| Question | Can we steer *toward/away from* a rhyme family? (behavioural) | Where/when does the model COMMIT to a specific rhyme? (mechanistic) |
| Intervention | continuous direction added at every forward step | discrete CLT suppress+inject at planning site once |
| Granularity | family-level ("any -ee word") | specific-word-level (P(" that") at trailing space) |
| Metric | generated-couplet last word in family list | P(specific alt word) at trailing-space spike |
| Falsifiability | weak (any movement counts) | strong (specific position, specific word, specific ratio) |
| Effect size | family rate ±20–30 pp | P(target) ×10⁵–10⁷ |

**Both can be true simultaneously.**  Maar's family-level direction in mid-layer residual stream does NOT contradict our Q1 negative result on specific-word redirection at the planning site.  Reviewers L1Vb02 + UvuC13 conflated the two; the rebuttal frame is: "different question, different protocol, different metric, both legitimate."

### 1d. The single-forward sweep's striking layer-0 effect is a different beast

Run #1 (single-forward sweep, unit-normalised direction) found `mean_p_target`
jumps from baseline `1.82 × 10⁻⁴` to `7.13 × 10⁻³` at L0/s=1.5
(**~39× baseline**), with hit_rate simultaneously dropping from 60% to 30% (HIT→MISS at L0/s=2).  This is REAL but it's measuring something different from Maar:
- the unit-normalised direction × m=1.5 has very different effective magnitude than Maar's raw `mean(pos) − mean(neg)` × m=1.5 (our unit × 1.5 ≈ 1.5 perturbation; Maar's raw × 1.5 ≈ 17 perturbation at this layer);
- the single-forward `mean_p_target` measures `P(" tree")` at the prompt's *last position* in a single forward pass — not the family-level last-word-of-generated-couplet metric Maar uses;
- Run #1's "L0 s=2 hit_rate = 30%" coincides numerically with Run #3's "60% → 30%" but is measuring an entirely different quantity (top-1-of-single-forward vs last-word-of-generation).

Run #1 is informative for the broader candle-mi steering surface but is **not** "Maar's protocol".

## 4. Two-paragraph rebuttal language (proposed)

> We agree Maar et al. (2026) report contrastive activation steering as effective for rhyme generation on a 23-model sweep.  Their documented protocol covers only Gemma 2 9B in the paper text; per-model specifications for the 1B–3B sizes our paper tests live in their OpenReview supplementary `.zip` (which requires authentication to download).  We downloaded their supplementary and ran their *exact* protocol — `0.8 × n_layers`, `m = 1.5`, raw (non-normalised) mean-difference vector, 25-token generated continuation, first-3-line last-word family-membership metric with `-ee` extended to `-y`/`-ee` endings — on Llama 3.2 3B using their *verbatim* test prompts.  We reproduce their qualitative finding: **baseline `-ee` rhyme rate 60% → steered 30%** (all 6 flips are HIT→MISS, 0 are MISS→HIT; 12/20 generated texts change under steering).  Our implementation is faithful and Maar's published claim about Llama 3.2 3B is reproducible.
>
> However, Maar's protocol and our paper's Q1/Q2 protocol answer different questions.  Maar steers a continuous direction at a fixed mid-late layer during 25-token generation, measuring whether the *generated couplet's last word is in a 30-word rhyme family list*.  Our Q1/Q2 measure whether suppressing the natural-rhyme CLT features and injecting a *specific alternative rhyme word's* CLT feature at the trailing-space planning site changes `P(specific alternative word)` by orders of magnitude.  These tasks differ in granularity (family-level vs specific-word), substrate (continuous direction vs sparse CLT feature), timing (every-step generation vs one-shot at planning site), and effect-size measurement (±30 pp vs ×10⁵).  Maar's positive result on family-level direction steering does not contradict our Q1 negative result on specific-word CLT redirection — both can be (and are) true.  The six methods we tested in Appendix A targeted the specific-word, planning-site task; Maar's family-level steering is a different and complementary tool.

## 6. Helper script for Maar→our-schema conversion

**Save as `scripts/convert_maar_prompts.py`** (per Commit 6 of the sequence in §5).  This is what produced the four `*_maar.json` files already on disk in `examples/results/maar_contrastive_steering/prompts/`; committing the script makes the provenance reproducible:

```python
import json, re
base = "examples/results/maar_contrastive_steering/maar_supp/supplementary_material/paper_experiments/data"
train = json.load(open(f"{base}/train/rhyme_family_lines.json", encoding="utf-8"))
test = json.load(open(f"{base}/test/rhyme_family_lines.json", encoding="utf-8"))
shared = open(f"{base}/../shared_utils.py", encoding="utf-8").read()
m = re.search(r"rhyme_family_words\s*=\s*(\{.*?^\})", shared, flags=re.DOTALL | re.MULTILINE)
literal = re.sub(r"\]\s*\)", "]", re.sub(r"set\(\s*\[", "[", m.group(1)))
rfw = eval(literal)

def convert(fam, contrast, path):
    words = sorted(rfw[fam])
    json.dump({
        "family": f"-{fam}", "template": "{line}\\n",
        "positive": train[fam], "negative": train[contrast],
        "eval": [
            {"prompt": line, "target_token": f" {words[0]}",
             "target_rhyme_words": [f" {w}" for w in words]}
            for line in test[fam]
        ],
        "source": "maar-supplementary",
        "source_url": "https://openreview.net/attachment?id=Z10pxu0Q7X&name=supplementary_material",
    }, open(path, "w", encoding="utf-8"), indent=2, ensure_ascii=False)

# -ee vs -oat for all three models
convert("ee", "oat", "examples/results/maar_contrastive_steering/prompts/llama32_3b_rhyme_ee_maar.json")
convert("ee", "oat", "examples/results/maar_contrastive_steering/prompts/llama32_1b_rhyme_ee_maar.json")
convert("ee", "oat", "examples/results/maar_contrastive_steering/prompts/gemma2_rhyme_ee_maar.json")
convert("oat", "ee", "examples/results/maar_contrastive_steering/prompts/gemma2_rhyme_oat_maar.json")
```

Note: there is NO `-out` family in Maar's data.  We use `-oat` as the `-ee` contrast (long-o vs long-e, phonologically distant).

## 11. Planning-disambiguation argument (for the rebuttal text)

This section is **rebuttal-text work, not v0.1.12-release work** — it captures
the argument developed in the final exchange before context compaction, so it
survives.

### Why L1Vb02 likely conflated the two protocols

Both papers have "planning" front-and-centre:
- Maar et al.: *"What's the plan? Metrics for implicit **planning** in LLMs..."*
- Our COLM submission: *"What Is the Minimum Architecture for **Prolepsis**?"* — but the abstract opens "We introduce **prolepsis**" and §2 is *"Planning in neural networks"*.

A reviewer skimming both sees "planning + rhyme + LLM" twice and pattern-matches them as the same research program.  Our "prolepsis" word choice was deliberately distinct from the AI-planning literature (search, plan synthesis, GTPyhop-style domain planning), but to a reviewer not steeped in that planning-research tradition, both papers read as "implicit planning during generation".  The Greek etymology (*prolepsis* = "anticipation" in classical rhetoric) was the right choice; it just needs to be **load-bearing** in the rebuttal, not assumed-known.

### Three rows to add to the §1c side-by-side comparison table

| Dimension | Maar's protocol | Our prolepsis protocol |
|---|---|---|
| **Sense of "planning"** | *Implicit planning* — "the model selects token N now in anticipation of token N+k" (behavioural anticipation, measured externally) | *Prolepsis* — "the model **commits** at early layers to a rhyme decision, and that commitment is **sustained** (not reversed) by later layers" (mechanistic structural motif: early commit + irrevocability) |
| **Where "planning" lives** | A residual-stream **direction** (continuous, distributed) that biases generation toward a rhyme family | A discrete **CLT feature activation** at a specific position + specific layer that subsequent layers cannot undo |
| **Adjacent literature** | Activation steering / representation engineering (Turner et al. 2023, Zou et al. 2023); behavioural-planning evaluation (Zhi-Xuan et al. 2025) | Early-commitment / irrevocability (Lehnert et al. 2024 trained search; Taufeeque et al. 2024 path channels); structural circuits (Nanda et al. 2023 grokking; Marks et al. 2024 sparse feature circuits) |

### Three reasons this helps the rebuttal

1. **Defuses the L1Vb02 confusion at source.**  Naming the senses explicitly (*implicit planning* vs *prolepsis as early-commit + sustained + irrevocable*) makes the conflation visible and forces a reviewer to argue against the specific distinction, not a vague "you do the same thing".
2. **Frames the contribution positively.**  The three properties — early commitment, sustained propagation through routing heads, zero correction — are the actual novelty.  Maar's work doesn't even attempt to measure irrevocability (their generation produces output, but they don't test whether late-layer counter-injection can reverse it).  That's the load-bearing distinction.
3. **Cites the adjacent literature each protocol descends from.**  Maar inherits from Turner-style steering; the COLM paper inherits from the grokking / circuit-discovery / trained-search line.  Different research programs, different ancestors, different success criteria.

### Suggested framing move at the rebuttal's first mention of "prolepsis"

Gloss it inline: **"prolepsis (early irrevocable commitment)"**.
