## 1. Headline result from v0.1.10 (the load-bearing finding for v0.1.11)

The Llama-vs-Gemma comparison under V3 Step 1.7's method-matched decoder-projection ranking gives a striking asymmetry:

| Model | CLT same-layer | CLT max-over-target | PLT same-layer | Step A (Jacopin hand-picked) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Llama 3.2 1B** | +5.7×10⁻⁷ (≈0) | **+0.871** | **+0.986** | +0.687 |
| **Gemma 2 2B** | +1.75×10⁻⁷ (≈0) | −1.69×10⁻⁹ (≈0) | +4.46×10⁻¹¹ (≈0) | +0.4567 |

The Llama 7-orders-of-magnitude ranking-method swing (5.7e-7 → 0.871 by switching CLT same-layer → max-over-target) **does not generalize**. On Gemma, *neither* method-matched ranking recovers the planning signal. Step A demonstrably controls it (P("around") = 0.4567), so planning is in the model — the ranking method is missing it.

**Outcome label for Gemma Step B:** degenerate Outcome B (both arms ≈ 0, same spike position 20 — `" in"`, mid-prompt). See [`docs/experiments/clt-vs-plt-planning-site/findings.md`](experiments/clt-vs-plt-planning-site/findings.md) for the full disambiguation between Step A (paper protocol, hand-picked features) and Step B (V3 Step 1.7 method-matched ranking) — that distinction is load-bearing.

## 2. v0.1.11 scope: faithful H&A protocol replication on open Llama 3.2 1B + Gemma 2 2B PLTs

### Why this experiment

If the Step B null on Gemma is **method-detectability** (Hanna & Ameisen's automatic feature-finding can't see the features either) or **model-capacity** (Gemma 2 2B genuinely lacks planning at this scale even though Step A's hand-picked features control it via some other mechanism), the answer matters for both:
- The COLM 2026 narrative (mechanism predicting breadth is the strongest move — see §4 below)
- The Hanna & Ameisen "planning emerges at 4B-8B" claim (their threshold may be method-dependent rather than model-dependent)

### Two H&A protocol differences from V3 Step 1.7

1. **Feature identification** — H&A use top-activating-token matching: a feature is a "rhyming feature" if ≥7 of its top-10 max-activating tokens share vowel + final consonant (CMUDict-decomposed), tokens are ≤5 chars, ≤5 activations per unique token. V3 Step 1.7 uses top-K by `cosine(decoder_row, unembed(planned_word))`.
2. **Intervention math** — H&A use multiplicative on activations (×7 to upweight, ×−3 to downweight). V3 Step 1.7 uses additive on decoder direction (zero to suppress, +10 to inject).

### Filter step ("does H&A's 985 land in our rhyme-group coverage?")

The user's COLM paper §3 establishes the CLT coverage boundary on each model:
- **Llama 3.2 1B CLT-524K:** 12 rhyme groups, 28 usable rhyme words
- **Gemma 2 2B CLT-426K:** 35 rhyme groups, ~98 usable words
- **Gemma 2 2B CLT-2.5M:** 209 individual rhyme words at decoder rank 1.0, zero cross-group contamination

H&A's 985 first-lines must be filtered: forward-pass each model on each first line to discover its natural rhyme, check whether that rhyme falls in our coverage, construct the suppress/inject feature set for the matching group. ~30 min compute, expected yield ~30-50% of 985 per model.

### Out of scope at this stage

- **a/an, is/are, el/la tasks** — your CLT-2.5M scan shows zero non-phonological features (Section 9 of your COLM paper). PLTs *might* have grammatical/syntactic features, but at our scales (~2B) it's empirically uncertain. One-off probe possible; not load-bearing.
- **Qwen-3 family scan** — would let us bridge directly into H&A's exact model family. Substantial new infrastructure (need Qwen-3 transformer support in candle-mi, new PLT loaders for `mwhanna/qwen3-*-transcoders*`). Was on the V3 Stage 2 roadmap; deferred.
- **General planning beyond rhyme + a/an-class tasks** — per the §3 CLT scan, the dictionaries we have don't carry domain features for code, cooking, or other non-phonological-distinctive domains. Same constraint as H&A.

## 3. The "What 'detection' means here" disambiguation (must survive into any v0.1.11 writeup)

Two distinct protocols inhabit this experiment family:

- **Step A (Jacopin paper protocol)** — hand-picked suppress + inject features chosen by domain expertise on rhyme-cluster geometry. `figure13_planning_poems.rs` reproduces this on both Llama 3.2 1B (P("that") = 0.687) and Gemma 2 2B (P("around") = 0.4567). **Step A confirms the planning signal exists and is causally controllable in both models.**
- **Step B (V3 Step 1.7 method-matched ranking)** — automatic top-5 by `cosine(decoder_row, unembed(planned_word))` at the inject feature's source layer. The point is *fairness*: score CLT and PLT by the same scoring procedure so the cross-transcoder-class comparison is apples-to-apples.

When Step B's ΔP is at noise level, the model has not lost its planning behaviour — Step A is the ground truth that the planning signal exists and is causally controllable. **A Step B null tells us about the protocol's sensitivity, not about model planning capacity.** This distinction is load-bearing for any conversation with Hanna & Ameisen.

## 4. Mechanism predicting breadth — the strongest possible synthesis

The user's research instinct (carried from 35 years of partial-order planning work) is **minimum-sufficient-structure-for-X**. The COLM paper's Q1-Q5 sequence is exactly that: each question identifies one more architectural property required for planning. Q4's "search ≤16 layers, commitment >16" is the modern direct analog of the partial-order planning community's classical results that completeness requires *this specific* mechanism, no more, no less.

Hanna & Ameisen are doing **phenomenology** — surveying planning behaviour across model scales without identifying the architectural minimum. The user's prolepsis paper is the **rigorous version** — bounding what's load-bearing, ablating what isn't, characterising what survives.

**The strongest possible move for v0.1.11 (and the COLM paper)** is mechanism *predicting* breadth: use the prolepsis framework to forecast what H&A's emergence curve must look like.

> "Commitment requires layer depth > 16; therefore any family below that depth must show no commitment regardless of parameter count, and any family above it should show commitment as soon as feature dictionaries are sufficient."

If that prediction held against a Pythia or open-LLaMA family scan, mechanism would *explain* breadth — and the v0.1.11 H&A engagement experiment becomes a falsification check on the prolepsis claim, not just a comparative benchmark. That's the framing that would seal the COLM narrative.

This isn't v0.1.11 scope (it'd need a Pythia/open-LLaMA family with CLTs available; we don't have those today). It's the "north star" the v0.1.11 work points toward.

## 6. The "SNLP / phenomenology vs rigor" framing (intellectual through-line, 1991 → 2026)

The user's 1991 AAAI submission was a French-mathematical-tradition theorem-first version of partial-order planning in Prolog (rejected). The accepted SNLP paper (McAllester & Rosenblitt 1991) had no correctness or completeness theorems — "it is possible to show that…" — but became canonical because it was published, not because it was rigorous.

35 years later, the same intellectual divide reappears: Hanna & Ameisen's paper is the "SNLP" of latent planning (a description of the phenomenon that hand-waves the mechanism); the user's prolepsis paper is the rigorous version (bounding what's load-bearing, ablating what isn't, characterising what survives). The discipline brought in 1991 is exactly what gives the 2026 paper its bite.

The 2025 twist: where 1991 planning was a *designed* algorithm (you could prove its correctness directly), 2025 planning is an *emergent* property of a trained net (you can only bound it empirically by showing what fails to plan). But the epistemic discipline — locate the boundary between "X cannot happen here" and "X happens here", characterize the load-bearing structural feature, prove necessity by ablation — is identical.

This framing should make it into the COLM-paper-related correspondence (and possibly the next v0.1.11 findings.md) as the **why** behind choosing minimum-architecture as the research target rather than scale-emergence phenomenology.
